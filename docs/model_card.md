# Model card: Call Severity Triage

Generated by `step10_safety.py` from the deployed model's metadata. Do not edit by hand.

## What it is
LightGBM classifier (T4_plus_force, 15 features) that estimates whether a road-traffic collision is
serious or fatal, from conditions known when the call is taken. Version `LightGBM-T4_plus_force-2021_2025-202611180414`.

## What it is not
It is not a model of emergency-call outcomes. It was trained on STATS19 police-attended injury collisions, so it only
sees collisions that were recorded, with the severity recorded later. It must not replace dispatcher judgement.

## Evidence (trained on 2021-2024, tested once on 2025)
| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Test calls | 101,525 |
| Share serious or fatal | 26.2% |
| ROC AUC | 0.627 |
| Precision in the top 2% | 55.4% (95% interval 53.2% to 57.5%) |
| Gain over the speed-limit baseline | 19.0 points (interval 16.0 to 21.8) |

An AUC near 0.63 is modest. Escalating the top 2% helps with ranking; it does not identify individual
serious collisions reliably.

## Behaviour checks
- Score stays between 0 and 1, is deterministic, ignores minutes within an hour, and treats blank optional fields as omitted
  (property tests in `test_safety.py`).
- Speed-limit sweep (everything else fixed): the score is higher at 60 mph than at 30 mph in 9 of 12
  contexts, and drops again at 70 mph in 8 of 12. The score is not monotone in the speed limit.
  This is what the data shows (70 mph roads are mostly motorways, where a smaller share of collisions is serious),
  so it is reported as a finding and not enforced as a rule. Full numbers are in `reports/safety_report.json`.

## Fairness slices (2025 replay, top 2% escalated nationally)
- Urban/rural recall: 0.4% to 10.0% (ratio 0.04).
- Police-force recall spans 0.0% to 36.8%
  across 25 forces with at least 1,500 test calls.
- Meaning: one national cutoff sends almost all escalations to rural areas and a few forces (Police Scotland most),
  and escalates almost nothing in urban areas. If each force has its own capacity, use a per-force cutoff
  (a quota of the top 2% inside each force) and report recall per force. Do not read the national figure as how
  well any one control room is served.
No single number makes a system fair. These are descriptive; differences can reflect real risk, reporting practice or both.

## Further experiments (reproduce with the step scripts)
- **Weather (step11, step12): no gain.** Adding observed weather for the collision hour and force changes P@2% by -0.2
  points (95% CI -1.1 to +1.0) and AUC by 0.0000. It is not in the model.
- **Exposure-adjusted hotspots (step13): modest improvement.** Dividing collision counts by traffic volume and testing
  cells with false-discovery control found 386 hotspots (naive counts: 349). Their 2024-25 serious/fatal rate per
  million vehicle-km was 7.7 times the rate elsewhere (95% CI 6.9 to 8.5), against 6.7 (6.0 to 7.5) for naive counts.
  Cells are chosen on 2021-23 and judged on 2024-25. Traffic volume is a modelled estimate, not a measurement.
- **Dispatch simulator robustness (step14): holds up.** Across 27 combinations of units, response time and patience
  (20 random shifts each), risk-ranked dispatch caught more serious/fatal calls than first come, first served in 25
  settings with a 95% interval above zero, and none worse. Median gain 7.7 points. The advantage vanishes when units
  can cover nearly every call (24 units, 20-minute responses: +0.5 points, interval includes zero), and shrinks when
  the age bonus for waiting calls is large (0.05: +3.5 points). The simulator is a model of dispatch, not evidence from
  real dispatch.
- **Cross-nation test (step15): ranking transfers, probabilities do not.** Trained on England 2021-24, tested on 2025.
  AUC is 0.59-0.61 in England, Wales and Scotland, and the model beats the speed-limit baseline in all three (lift at
  the 2% flag rate 1.4-1.5 against 1.2-1.3 for Wales and Scotland). Predicted risk is 9 points too low in Wales and
  18 points too low in Scotland, whose serious/fatal share (44%) is far higher than England's (25%), probably partly
  because severity is recorded differently. Use the score to rank calls in Wales and Scotland, and recalibrate before
  quoting a probability there.
- **Handover brief:** the text is built from computed shift figures. A language-model rewrite is shown only if every
  number in it matches those figures; otherwise the plain template is used.

## Known limits
Recording practice differs by force and nation; severity labels can change after the call; the model does not see
vehicles, casualties, time-critical symptoms or traffic volume. Northern Ireland is not in this data and has not been
tested.
